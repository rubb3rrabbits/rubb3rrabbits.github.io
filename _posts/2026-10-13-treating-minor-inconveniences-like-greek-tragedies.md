The ancient Greeks understood hubris, but they never experienced a belt loop catching on a kitchen doorknob.

Dropping a slice of buttered toast face down is not merely a kinetic accident; it is the deliberate intervention of Apollo reminding you of your mortal frailty. The slow, catastrophic realization that the milk turned sour precisely midway through pouring it over expensive granola is an act of betrayal worthy of Euripides.

We fall to our knees on the linoleum, demanding an explanation from Olympus, while the toaster clicks mockingly.
