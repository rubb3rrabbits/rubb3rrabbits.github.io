Every modern dwelling, regardless of architectural pedigree or square footage, contains a designated void: the junk drawer. It is an archaeological sediment layer of human compromise.

Inside lies a standard inventory: three loose AA batteries (charge status: indeterminate), an unidentifiable brass key that likely unlocks a chest left behind in 1994, soy sauce packets hardened into fossilized amber, and half a roll of electrical tape encrusted with stray lint.

We do not clean the junk drawer. We merely visit it to pay tribute, search for scissors we know are not there, and close it quickly before its contents achieve critical mass.
